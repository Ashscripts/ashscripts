The Scene

The Scene Monday morning. Phase 6. Ashpak arrives to find a spreadsheet on his desk from Riya. Subject line: "App Audit; Action Required." He opens it:

At the bottom, Riya has written in red pen:

"We are paying for 300 Microsoft 365 licences that are assigned manually, one by one. Copilot licences are assigned to people who left the company. We have no idea what's running on our devices. Fix this."

Ashpak puts the spreadsheet down.

"App management. This is the messiest part of any Intune deployment. Let's build it properly."

The same app can have multiple assignment types for different groups:

1 Microsoft Teams: 2 Required → INTUNE-DEV-WINDOWS-CORPORATE 3 (all corporate Windows laptops) 4 Available → INTUNE-DEV-WINDOWS-BYOD 5 (BYOD users can install if they want) 6 Uninstall → INTUNE-GRP-Contractors-Offboarded 7 (remove from contractor devices at offboarding)

App Deployment Flow How apps get from Intune to devices:

The Company Portal; User's App Store The Company Portal app is the user-facing interface for Intune app management. It serves two purposes:

Company Portal must be deployed to all devices:

1 intune.microsoft.com 2 └── Apps → All apps 3 └── [Search: Company Portal] 4 → Microsoft Intune Company Portal 5 → Assign 6 Required: INTUNE-DEV-ALL 7 (every enrolled device 8 gets Company Portal)

App Reporting in Intune Ashpak monitors app deployment across the fleet:

1 intune.microsoft.com 2 └── Apps → Monitor 3 └── Discovered apps 4 (Shows every app found on every 5 managed device; not just IT-deployed) 6 7 App Devices Version 8 Microsoft Teams 812 24.x.x 9 Google Chrome 634 120.x 10 VLC Media Player 203 3.x 11 Steam 47 ← flag this 12 TikTok 23 ← flag this 13 WhatsApp Business 156 ← review

Discovered apps is the answer to Riya's audit finding. Intune automatically inventories every app on every managed device. Ashpak uses this report to identify unapproved apps and create uninstall assignments for them.

App Protection Policies (MAM); Preview App Protection Policies (APP); also called MAM policies; are the data protection layer for apps. They're covered fully in Lesson 6.6, but Ashpak introduces the concept now:

App Protection Policies work with or without device enrollment; which is why the CA-CORP- CompliantOrMAM-BYOD policy from Lesson 5.6 works for unmanaged devices.

Lesson 6.2: Deploying Microsoft 365 Apps: Ashpak pushes Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams to 800 laptops silently :

The Scene Monday late morning. The app audit spreadsheet is still on Ashpak's screen. He filters to the most critical gap:

127 Windows laptops don't have the full Microsoft 365 suite. Users are working with older Office versions, some even Office 2016. 31 MacBooks are in the same state. 203 more devices have M365 but haven't updated in months; security vulnerabilities, feature gaps, compatibility issues. Riya's note from the audit: "We pay for M365 E3 licences for everyone. Every laptop should have the apps. Why don't they?" Because nobody deployed them centrally. Each employee either had Office pre-installed from the manufacturer, downloaded it themselves, or went without. No standardisation. No version control. No central management. Ashpak is about to change that.

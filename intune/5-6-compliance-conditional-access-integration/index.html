The Scene

Friday morning. All compliance policies are live: 316 non-compliant devices. All of them are still accessing SharePoint, Outlook, Teams, and the CRM without restriction. Kabir walks past the screen:

"Wait; those 316 non-compliant devices can still access everything?" "For now," says Ashpak. "Compliance policies tell us who fails. Conditional Access is what actually blocks them. Without CA, compliance is just a report."

He opens a new tab. entra.microsoft.com . Conditional Access.

"Now we make it mean something."

What Is Conditional Access?

Conditional Access is Entra ID's (Azure AD's) policy engine. CA Policy 1: Require Compliant Device for All Corporate Apps

The foundational policy: any managed device accessing corporate resources must be compliant. Assignments:

Break-glass accounts are critical. If Conditional Access is misconfigured and blocks all

access; including admins; you need break-glass accounts to get back in. These accounts: Have no MFA or device compliance requirement Passwords stored in a physical safe Usage triggers immediate security alerts Reviewed and tested quarterly Never create a CA policy that includes break-glass accounts. Legacy authentication protocols (IMAP, POP3, SMTP AUTH, basic auth) bypass MFA and Conditional Access. Block them entirely:

CA Policy 3: Require MFA for All Users

Compliance-based access + MFA = defence in depth:

CA Policy 4: High Risk Sign-In Block

Entra ID Protection assigns risk scores to sign-in attempts. Block high-risk sign-ins entirely: 1 Name: CA-CORP-RequireMFA-AllUsers 3 USERS: All users 4 Exclude: Break-glass accounts 6 TARGET RESOURCES: All cloud apps 8 CONDITIONS: Not configured (apply always) 10 GRANT: 11 ● Grant access 12 Require multifactor authentication 14 Enable policy: On

What triggers High sign-in risk?

These are detected by Microsoft's threat intelligence and Entra ID Protection. A blocked high-risk sign-in means an attacker with valid credentials still can't get in.

CA Policy 5: Require Compliant Device or MAM for BYOD

MAM without MDM: This policy means a contractor on their personal MacBook can still

access SharePoint; but only through a managed app (Outlook, Edge, Teams) with an App Protection Policy applied. The data is protected within the app even though the device isn't enrolled. This is how AshScript handles contractors, vendors, and unmanaged personal devices.

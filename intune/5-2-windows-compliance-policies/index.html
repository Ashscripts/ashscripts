The Scene

Same Wednesday. 11:30 AM. Ashpak has the compliance policy framework clear in his mind. Now he builds the actual rules; starting with Windows, which covers 800 of AshScript's 847 enrolled devices. He opens a document and lists what a compliant AshScript Windows laptop looks like: 1 ASHSCRIPT COMPLIANT WINDOWS DEVICE: 2 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────── 3 Windows 10 21H2 minimum (or Windows 11) 4 BitLocker enabled on OS drive 5 Secure Boot enabled 6 Code Integrity (HVCI) enabled 7 Microsoft Defender active and up-to-date 8 Firewall enabled 9 No high or critical active threats 10 Password/PIN required to sign in 11 Device not jailbroken or tampered with 12 Checked in with Intune within 30 days

This list becomes the compliance policy.

Defender risk score integration: MDE continuously analyses device telemetry and assigns a

risk score. Intune compliance policy checks this score. If a device has an active malware infection (High risk), it immediately becomes non-compliant; Conditional Access blocks it from corporate resources even if every other compliance check passes. This is real-time threat-based access control.

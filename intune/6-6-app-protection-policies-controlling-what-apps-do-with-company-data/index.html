The Scene

The Scene Thursday morning. All apps are deploying. Ashpak is about to close his laptop when a message arrives from Riya:

"Security incident. One of our Sales reps left last month. Before she left, she forwarded 47 client emails from Outlook to her personal Gmail. We only found out because a client complained. Her phone was her own; we never managed it. We have no record of what she took. Legal is involved."

Ashpak stares at the screen. The phone was personal. Never enrolled in Intune. No configuration profiles. No compliance policy. No device management at all. Outlook was installed from the App Store. The company data; every email, every attachment, every contact; sat inside Outlook completely unprotected. She could do anything with it. Forward it. Screenshot it. Save it to personal iCloud. Copy it to WhatsApp. All of it. All undetected. He opens a new browser tab.

"This stops today."

He navigates to Intune App Protection Policies. This is the tool that would have made every one of those 47 forwarded emails impossible; without anyone ever touching her personal phone.

What Are App Protection Policies? App Protection Policies (APP); also called MAM (Mobile Application Management) policies; control what managed apps can do with company data. They work at the app level, not the device level:

What APP Policies Can Protect Against

APP Architecture: How It Works

Which apps support APP policies? Only Microsoft Intune SDK-integrated apps support APP policies. These include all Microsoft 365 apps (Outlook, Teams, Edge, OneDrive, SharePoint, Word, Excel, PowerPoint) and many third-party apps (Salesforce, Adobe Acrobat, ServiceNow,

Box). Non-SDK apps cannot be managed by APP; which is why the CA policy requires approved client apps for BYOD access.

"Disabled account → Wipe org data" is the answer to the data breach scenario at the start of this lesson. When the Sales rep's account was disabled in Entra ID (as it should have been on her last day), this conditional launch rule would have automatically wiped all company data from every managed app on her personal phone; without IT doing anything. The 47 forwarded emails would never have happened if APP was configured.

Troubleshooting APP Policy Issues

Issue 1: APP Policy Not Applying to User

1 SYMPTOM: User reports no PIN prompt in Outlook 2 Company data flows freely between apps 3 4 CAUSE: APP policy not reaching the user 5 6 DIAGNOSIS: 7 intune.microsoft.com 8 → Apps → App protection status 9 → iOS user status 10 Search: user@ashscript.com 11 Policy assigned? /× 12 Checked in? /× 13 14 COMMON CAUSES: 15 × User not in assigned group 16 × User hasn't signed into managed app yet 17 (policy applies at FIRST sign-in) 18 × App version too old 19 (very old Outlook may not support SDK) 20 21 FIX: 22 Check user's group membership 23 Ask user to sign out and back into Outlook 24 Check Outlook version; update if needed 25 Force policy refresh: 26 User opens Company Portal → Sync

Issue 2: Users Complain PIN Is Too Restrictive

1 SYMPTOM: Users complain about constant PIN 2 prompts; reducing productivity 3 4 CONTEXT: Recheck access after 30 minutes 5 means frequent PIN entry 6 7 BALANCE ADJUSTMENT: 8 Increase offline grace period: 720 → 1440 min 9 Increase recheck time: 30 → 60 minutes 10 Enable Face ID / Touch ID: 11 (Already enabled; ensure users know 12 they can use biometrics) 13 14 POLICY UPDATE: 15 IOS-APP-Corporate-All → Edit 16 Recheck access requirements: 60 minutes 17 Offline grace period: 1440 minutes (24h) 18 → Save 19 20 NOTE: Reducing security is always a 21 management decision; document it 22 Balance: security vs usability

Issue 3: "Open In" Prompt Allows Personal App Access

1 SYMPTOM: iOS "Open In" / share sheet shows 2 personal apps when sharing from Outlook 3 User can open attachment in personal 4 Files app 5 6 CAUSE: APP policy restricts data transfer 7 but iOS share sheet may still show 8 unmanaged apps; some versions allow 9 opening with user override 10 11 STRONGER FIX: 12 Send org data to other apps: 13 Change: Policy managed apps 14 To: Policy managed apps with 15 Open-In/Share filtering 16 17 This uses iOS MDM to filter share sheet ;

18 shows ONLY managed apps in Open-In list 19 Requires: Supervised device (ADE enrolled) 20 21 FOR UNSUPERVISED/BYOD: 22 Cannot filter share sheet via MDM 23 APP blocks actual data transfer even if 24 share sheet shows unmanaged apps 25 (App opens but shows no content)

Issue 4: Selective Wipe After Account Disabled

1 SCENARIO: User account disabled but company 2 data still showing in Outlook 3 on personal device 4 5 CAUSE: Conditional launch "disabled account 6 → wipe" requires app to check in 7 Device may be offline 8 9 TIMELINE: 10 Account disabled in Entra 11 → Next time Outlook opens and checks in 12 → Policy evaluates: account disabled 13 → Wipe triggered 14 15 IF DEVICE IS OFFLINE/APP NOT OPENED: 16 Wipe happens next time app opens 17 Can take hours to days 18 19 MANUAL FORCE: 20 intune.microsoft.com 21 → Apps → App protection status 22 → [user] → Wipe 23 Immediately queues wipe of all 24 company data from managed apps 25 when device next checks in
